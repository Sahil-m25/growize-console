The Growize Test Book

Hand this to a tester and they can work without asking a question. Every case says why it exists — the decision behind it and what goes wrong without it — then how to set up, what to do, what to expect, and what to check behind the screen. The work is broken into 22 phases: the lead console first (48 cases), then the Investor Management portal (42), then the seam between them, the investor app and the cutover. 104 test cases, 69 of them blockers, 87 unit tests named for the people who build each phase, and seven acceptance scripts walked as a working day.

Two tracks: Business tester works only in the portal; Technical tester checks the database, the events, the audit log and the alerts. Cases marked with both are run together. 0 of 104 run

How to use this

read once, then work the phases in order

Work one phase at a time, in order. A phase has an entry condition, a list of what is real and what is still stubbed, its unit tests, its cases, and an exit condition. Do not start a phase before its entry condition is true — most false defects come from testing something that was not built yet. Do not carry a blocker or a major defect into the next phase.

Every case has a “why”. That paragraph is the decision the case is defending, in plain words. Read it before the steps: it tells you what the system is supposed to be doing, so when something looks odd you can tell a defect from a design you did not know about. If you disagree with the why, that is worth raising — it means the design and the expectation have drifted, which is a defect of a more expensive kind.

Record a result on every case you run — pass, fail, blocked or skipped — with a note. Blocked means something outside the case stopped you; skipped means it does not apply yet. Results are shared with everyone who opens this page, so two testers never repeat each other's work.

Raise a defect the moment you see one, using the template below, and put the defect number in the note. A defect described a day later is a defect somebody has to reproduce twice.

The one rule that is not negotiable. No test ever runs against real investor data, a real bank account, a real PAN or a real signature — including your own. Every environment in the table below is a sandbox or a branch. If a case seems to need real data, stop and ask; it does not.

Twelve rules a tester needs to know

the decisions behind the cases

These are the design decisions that explain most of what you will see. Each one says what to expect on screen, so that correct-but-surprising behaviour is not reported as a defect — and so that the absence of it is.

One fact, one writer

Every fact in this system is written in exactly one place by exactly one role. Zoho is the book; Supabase is the mirror the app reads.

if you can change the same number from two screens, that is a defect, even if both screens agree.
The IR owns the lead; Finance confirms

Nothing is ever transferred. The IR moves the lead up the ladder; Finance's confirmations open the gates that let them.

an IR blocked at a rung with a message naming the confirmation they are waiting for — not a way round it.
Recording is always allowed; matching is what the rules gate

Money that arrives must always be recordable. The controls sit on matching it, not on knowing about it.

you can always enter money that has arrived, and you often cannot match it yet.
A gate opens on a fact and closes on its reversal

Gates used only to open. Reversal events now close them and step the ladder back.

after any reversal, the ladder, the funnel and the app all move back together — not one of them.
One consequence, one actor

Each outcome — an acknowledgement, a hold, an account opening — is caused by exactly one thing.

exactly one email, one hold, one invite. Two is a defect even when both are correct.
A system touch is never a human touch

Automated mail is visible and never counts towards a conversation with an investor.

the touch count moves only when a person did something.
Identity is unreadable by opening a record

PAN, Aadhaar and bank details are encrypted, hidden by field-level security, and revealed only through a logged request with a reason.

masked values everywhere, and a log entry every time one is shown.
The app never shows a number it cannot stand behind

Money figures carry the time they were true and are withheld when the mirror is stale.

‘being updated’ rather than an old number — that is correct behaviour, not a bug.
The seam is idempotent

Every event carries an id. Repeats, retries and replays land exactly once.

doing the same thing twice by accident produces one record, not two.
Every clock is computed in Indian time from the event

Holds, FEMA, DPDP, reminders and refunds all come from one function in Asia/Kolkata.

a deadline that lands on the same calendar day everywhere you look, including at 23:30.
Nothing moves without an event

State changes are caused by typed events, not by someone editing a field.

a history on every record that explains how it got where it is.
An approval is never self-approved

Every approval names a group with an alternate, and the submitter can never be the approver.

a refusal when one person tries to do both — including when they are the most senior person present.

An IR signs in with their own Zoho account and lands on their own day

Business testerblocker if it fails

One identity per human is the whole attribution model (D6). If the portal had its own passwords, Zoho’s audit log would name the wrong person on every write, and offboarding would be two jobs instead of one.

Set up

Priya (IR) exists as an active user in the Leads sandbox org with the IR profile. She has never signed in to the portal.

Steps
  1. Open the console URL in a private window.
  2. Choose Sign in with Zoho.
  3. Approve the consent screen the first time it appears.
  4. Land on the console.
Expect

  • After approval the console shows Priya’s name and the IR menu.
  • ‘My day’ is the first page and it lists only Priya’s leads.
Also check — the side effects
  • Sign in again in a second private window: no second consent screen appears (one refresh token per person).
  • In Zoho, Setup → Audit log shows the sign-in against Priya, not against the integration user.

Technical testermajor if it fails

Where this comes from. Every case is traceable to The Growize Build Book — the section number, the decision (D1–D20) and, where the case exists because of a known failure mode, that mode's id in Appendix A of the same book. The register of fixes (Appendix B) is the work list those cases verify. Nothing here invents a requirement: if a case seems to test something the Build Book does not say, that is a defect in one of the two documents and should be raised as one.
