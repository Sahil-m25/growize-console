# D35 — Four destinations become two doors, a drawer and a collapse

_14 Sep 2026 · follows D34 · the owner's instruction was "the lesser the pages, the better"_

D33 and D34 cut what was *on* each page. This cuts the pages. Two destinations came off the rail
entirely, and neither lost a capability on the way out — which is the test this decision had to pass.
A page you remove by deleting the work it held is not simplification, it is loss.

## Add lead was a page holding a form that had two other entrances

The top-bar button and the `add.quick` door both render `vAdd()`. A third way in, occupying a row on
every seat's rail, answered a question the other two had already answered. It is off `NAV` and out of
the router; `vAdd()` survives as the door's body and nothing else calls it.

`add` stays in `SEATSCREENS` and `PAGECAPS`, marked `nopage`. This is the subtle part: reach is what
the capability check reads, so a right held on nothing you can navigate to is still a right, and
`capture` is exactly that — the right to put a name on the book, wherever the form was opened from.
The marker keeps it out of every place reach is counted or drawn as a screen.

The removal broke the thing it was meant to serve, and two verification agents found it
independently: `capb.hidden` was computed from `navFor().some(n=>n.k==="add")`, which the same change
made permanently false, and the stylesheet paints `.btn` over the browser's own `[hidden]` rule. So
every seat — including the four that may not capture — got a capture shortcut that the gate believed
it had hidden. The gate now asks the capability, `[hidden]` is declared `!important` once for the
whole stylesheet, and the shortcut stands down while its own door is open.

## Team & access held four things and the console read one of them

Availability is read in twenty-nine places — cover, the secondary owner, the out-tag beside a name,
the amber on a lead whose owner is away. It already had two other homes: the people count in the top
bar and the roster panel on Profile. So the page could go and availability could stay, and it did.

The access grid moved into the `person` drawer, which the presence list now opens: a manager's row is
a button, everybody else's row is a row. The grid is opened a few times a year; it did not need a
destination, and putting it one tap from the count of who is in puts it beside the other thing you do
to a person.

The org chart and the lent-access list went. Nothing in the console read either. What went with them:
`vPeople`, `vRoster`, `vTeams`, `vMembers`, `addPerson`, `removePerson`, `setMgr`, `setSeat`,
`seatClash`, `moveCost`, `clashOf`, `teamsList`, `teamOfPerson` and the add-member drawer.

Temporary access stayed, because `log()` and `navFor()` read it — but lending had been left with no
way back once the page that listed grants was gone. A loan you cannot revoke is not a loan. The
person drawer now shows what is lent to somebody and takes it back.

One thing the sweep caught while the grid was being rehoused: the Operations Lead, whom the manual
describes as view-only, held `seats` — the sharpest permission in the console, the one that changes
what another person's console will refuse. She keeps `roster`; `seats` now belongs to the manager who
carries the book and to Digital Infrastructure.

## Many leads at once, and the event they came from

The owner asked for bulk capture and said that when it is many, they have to be tagged to an event.
Half of that already existed and nobody could find it: `loadSheet(ev)` creates a whole event's rows
in one pass and tags every one by construction. It is now offered from the capture door.

A file the owner already has is not an event sheet, so there is a second path: a `.csv`, read in the
page with `FileReader` — no network, nothing leaves the browser — that previews before it writes. The
preview says how many rows parsed, which column it matched to which field, which rows are unusable
and why, and which are already on the book. **An event is required**, and nothing writes until one is
chosen: a name with no source cannot be credited to anything or costed against anything.

Both paths end at `putLead`, factored out of `addLead` so capture keeps one writer. The import runs
inside one `commit()`, so it lands whole or not at all.

Imported leads arrive **unconsented**. The event sheet may mark its rows consented because the intake
form asked the person standing there; a spreadsheet column is the file's word, not the investor's,
and consent in this console is a channel, a how, a minute and a name — none of which a file supplies.
Outbound stays blocked until an IR records it.

## The rail retracts

240px to 68px, icons only, every destination still in the tab order with its accessible name and its
badge at full size — a badge shrunk into the corner of an icon stops being a number, and the red one
is the only thing in the rail with a claim on somebody. Desktop only: below 901px the rail is already
a horizontal strip and the control is not drawn. The state survives a redraw and a navigation, and is
reset by `endSession()`, because a console that opens half-shut for a reason the next person did not
choose is furniture from a stranger.

*Consequence for the port.* Two routes disappear. `capture`, `seats` and `roster` become permissions
without pages, which is what they always were. The CSV parser is the only new thing the port cannot
lift unchanged — it reads a file in the browser, and the real build will want the same preview before
the same server-side write.
