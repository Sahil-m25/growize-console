# D21 — KYC is a switch a person sets, money is typed and checked against the bank statement weekly, and the Leads org holds four or five seats — not thirteen

_7 Sep 2026 · Q9 and Q24 closed · Appendix C revised_

Added 7 Sep 2026 on three clarifications from the tech lead. **KYC:** no provider integration. Documents are uploaded and Compliance sets the state by hand — passed, failed, frozen — logged with who, when and against which documents. Every state, gate and downstream refusal in §6, §10 and §21 is unchanged; only the thing that sets the state changes, and the provider can be added later without touching the model (Q24 closed for now). **Money:** no bank credit feed and no virtual accounts (Q9 closed for now). Finance Operations records each receipt — date, amount, mode, UTR, investor, purpose — and the Head of Finance confirms it; maker-checker is unchanged. The weekly **statement upload** is kept and is now the primary control rather than a second opinion: every line in the bank statement is matched to a receipt or listed with an owner. **Why:** at a few credits a week the feed buys nothing that a person is not already doing, and it costs four to eight weeks of the bank's process; the statement gives the same certainty for the price of an upload, and the feed slots in later with the statement still checking it. **Seats:** the Leads org holds two or three IRs and one manager on full seats, one view-only seat, and the integration user. Finance and marketing hold no Leads seat at all; the founder, tech, the BU owner and operations read the master dashboard, where leads are numbers, sources and events rather than people with owners. **Cost:** the Leads org falls from thirteen seats to about six.
