# D36 — Teams comes back, and four things get easier to read

_14 Sep 2026 · corrects D35 · "I wanted you to remove the people's page and not the teams page"_

D35 read "the people's page" as the whole of **Team & access**, which was one destination with four
tabs: Availability, Teams, Members, Temporary access. Three of those four were the owner's, and they
should not have gone. They are back.

What stays out is **Availability**, and that is the part the instruction was actually about: it is
read twenty times a day and changed once, so it belongs to the people count in the top bar where it
is already in front of everybody, not to a tab somebody has to travel to. The rest — the shape of the
team, the people in it, what each may do, what has been lent — is visited on purpose and changed
deliberately, and earns a destination. The page is now called **Teams**, with three tabs.

The lesson worth keeping: a page named for one thing can hold four, and "remove X" is not a safe
instruction to act on until you have said out loud what X contains. Asking cost one message; not
asking cost a round trip and a page.

## What the restore broke, and how it was caught

Splicing deleted code back is not free, and two of the three defects came from the seam:

- **`grantTemp` and `revokeTemp` were never restored.** Three call sites, no definitions — lending a
  screen and taking it back both threw. The feature looked whole because the tab rendered.
- **The Position select wrote to the wrong person.** `setSeat(this.value)` acted on `PSEL`, the row
  the Members list last opened, while the drawer it sits in can be opened from four other places that
  never touch `PSEL`. Open Jhalak from Members, close, open Rohit from the top-bar count, change
  Rohit's position — and Jhalak was silently reseated, with the log line naming her. The person is an
  argument now; `PSEL` is only a fallback. It also goes through `commit()` and carries `about`, so
  the person hears about it on their own Updates like a grid change does.
- **`moveCost` was defined twice** — the seam itself.

And one that predated the restore: **four places counted "how many screens does this person reach"
and three counted the capability groups that are not screens**, so one person read as 9, 10 and 10 on
a single visit. `screensOf()` and `screenCount()` are now the only two answers.

## My day says what kind of work a row is

The owner: *"everything looks the same — documents, money, which stage I'm in."*

`nextUp()` now returns a **kind** alongside what it already returned, and there are nine: touch,
paperwork, money, stage, next step, no next step, owner, waiting on Finance, nothing outstanding.
Each carries three cues — a glyph, a quiet ground, and a word — because colour alone is not a cue.

**Urgency keeps the left edge; kind takes the glyph and the wash.** How soon a row is due is the only
thing this queue is ever sorted by, and an edge that sometimes meant "money" and sometimes "late"
would answer neither question. Only the four chases — paper, money, stage, touch — carry the row
wash: tint every row and you have tinted none. Verified distinct under `grayscale(1)`, so shape
carries the kind when colour cannot, and every tile has an accessible name.

The same vocabulary draws in all three places it appears: the queue, the shortest-route panel, and
the book's Next action column. It pays best in the panel, where three rows that all read "money in —
finish it" are now visibly a stage move, a Finance wait and a money chase.

## One line of filters

Nothing was removed — the instruction was explicit about that. The eight hygiene chips became one
**Needs attention** select beside Stage, which uses the page's own control vocabulary and reads the
same `exc` array the book rail is cut from, so no count is recomputed. The at-a-glance scan of all
eight is not lost: the rail still prints it beside the table.

A cut that is on but whose control cannot show it — the source, or an owner the select has no option
for — appears as a pill on the row and clears in one press. One row at 1440, 1366 and 1280; below
1180 the rail drops under the table and the row is no longer competing for a line.

## Events: create, change, remove — and who works them

`addEvent()` used to write a canned row. One drawer now adds, corrects and removes.

What stays editable after an event has run: its name, kind, channel, dates, city, cost and who worked
it — a record nobody may correct goes stale and gets kept in a spreadsheet instead. Two things do not
move: the **id**, because a lead still names it and handing it to another event silently re-credits
that lead's night and spend; and **"it ran"**, because reversing it takes the event out of every
table that counts what events produced while its leads go on carrying its tag. The tablet count is
floored at the leads actually tagged, or the page reads "tagged 31 of 20".

**Deleting an event with leads tagged** asks first and names the count. The leads stay — owners,
stages, history, receipts untouched, still sourced "Events". What they lose is the night. No lead is
ever left pointing at a record that is gone, and an id is never re-issued.

**The IRs who work an event** are named on it, and when its sheet loads or a CSV is imported against
it, the new leads are dealt round-robin among them and land already owned. The split is shown before
it writes — "31 leads — Rohit 11, Kavya 10, Nikhil 10" — and the preview is the write run once,
walking the same rows through the same function, so the two cannot disagree. A row refused as a
duplicate is removed before the deal, not out of somebody's share. Each assignment writes the same
log line a manual pick-up writes, so Activity and the capture credit stay true. Name nobody and the
old behaviour is exactly unchanged: the leads land unowned and are picked up from the waiting card.

*Consequence for the port.* One route returns. `setSeat` taking its subject as an argument is the
shape every per-person write should have had; `screensOf`/`screenCount` are the shape every count
should have. The round-robin is the first place this console deals work out rather than recording
work already done — the real build will want the same preview-equals-write guarantee.
