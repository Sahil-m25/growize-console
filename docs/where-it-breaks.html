Where It Breaks

Every place the design as written can halt, go wrong, or fail without telling anyone — with the chance of each in the first year, what would notice it today, and what should. 145 modes; 27 are near-certain; 53 are silent; 40 carry a legal edge; the book’s six alerts see 11 of them in time. The verdict is not that the design is fragile — it is that its watchers watch themselves, its gates only open, and its approvals are people. Read the five patterns first; most of the register is one of them.

The verdict, in five sentences

honest, and in order of what to fix

It will break in the first month, in five known places, and all five are one-line design changes: money is refused before verification, a full payment fits no gate, KYC has no step, every approval is one person, and the BU Owner has no seat.

The dangerous failures are the silent ones, and they share five shapes: gates that only open, two actors per consequence, one named person everywhere, watchers that watch themselves, and a reconcile that compares records while the failures live between records.

The six alerts are the wrong six. They see 11 of 145 modes in time; eleven canaries — one of them off-platform — see 88, and a design change removes 40 more.

Nothing here argues against the base. Zoho primary, two orgs, two projects and eMudhra all stand; what changes is reversal events, one actor per fact, delegation, a KYC step, a job runner, an allow-list mapper and a heartbeat that lives somewhere else.

Two items are due before any of it: the licence lapses on 13 September, and the securities opinion still has no date.

The ten most dangerous silent failures

ranked by damage × silence
  1. Money the ledger cannot seeMO5MO6

    a credit before the Contact exists, to the wrong account, or whose webhook was missed never becomes a Receipt; ‘Money waiting’ only sees Receipts that exist. Nothing in the book compares the bank statement to the ledger.

  2. Money gated on an instrument nobody readGA6

    §21 auto-verifies when certificate and hash agree; §12 says a human checks it is the right document. Wrong units, price or name gets verified and opens the money gate.

  3. Identity leaks back into the mirrorMI7

    the mapper strips named fields; the next field anyone adds in Zoho is mirrored into jsonb by default. The leak the portal was built to close, reopened by a picklist.

  4. Fully paid, never allottedGA5

    auto-allot is evaluated on whichever fact fires it; KYC passing a week later re-evaluates nothing; the investor sits in no queue.

  5. Gates that only openGA2

    a reversed receipt, a blocked document or a refund never nulls a gate column; there are no reversal events. Reserved without money, forever, and a funnel that counts the refund as a sale.

  6. Units changed after handoverMO13

    units_intended edits on the Lead; due is computed on the frozen Contact; the balance never matches, or the wrong units are allotted at the wrong price.

  7. Residency and the freezeKY4KY3

    an NRI typed as resident is allotted with no FEMA record and no filing; a frozen investor is paid because payouts read the bank’s beneficiary, not Zoho’s flag.

  8. Alerts made by the thing they watchSE2SE1

    the six alerts run on the cron in the project they monitor. When pg_cron stops — after a restore, an upgrade, a looping job — the alerts stop with it.

  9. Transitions the reconcile cannot seeGA12MI10

    a dropped handover or gate webhook is not a field difference; a reconcile that dies at the function limit reports success. Both look like a quiet night.

  10. Two records for one personHO3HO6

    a partial handover retried, or a returning investor, creates a second Contact; holdings double or halve; nothing asserts one Lead per Contact or one uid per Contact.
