How to use this document

A new session needs nothing but this file. Read §2 for what the business does, §3 for where each fact lives, §18 for why every decision was taken and what was rejected, §19 for what is still open, and §22 for how it breaks. Everything else is reference for the part you are building. The three appendices are the detail: A is the failure register in full (145 modes with chances, detection and fixes), B is the work list in full (156 items in fourteen segments — the thing to work through), C is what it costs to run and what an agent can build through the CLI and MCP.

The book is now the repository’s docs/ folder. Everything here ships inside one repo — the console, the portal, the app, the contracts, the migrations and the ops all beside it, folders rather than submodules (§23). §24 is the diagnostic that reads the running system every morning and explains what it found without touching it; §25 is how the agent that writes most of this works, and what it is forbidden to do. If you are an agent, CLAUDE.md at the repository root comes before this file — it is shorter, and it tells you which part of this one to open.

The dates are their own page. Ninety Days, Three Portals puts this book's phases on a calendar — thirteen weeks from 7 September to 4 December 2026, the console first, the portal second, the app re-pointed, the migration rehearsed twice — with testing interleaved a week behind building, three month gates, the external lead times that cannot be compressed, and the order things are cut if it runs late. It is the only one of the four documents with dates on it, so it is the one that changes when reality moves.

Testing is its own book. The Growize Test Book takes the same design and turns it into 22 testing phases — the lead console first, then the Investor Management portal, then the seam, the app and the cutover — with 104 test cases, 87 unit tests and seven acceptance scripts. Every case carries the decision it defends, so a tester can tell a defect from a design they had not met. Hand that book to testers; hand this one to builders.

The live copies. Two companion pages hold the same content in a form you work in rather than read: Eighty-Two Fixes, One Book (Appendix B with shared ticks and a note beside each item, so two people see the same state) and Where It Breaks (Appendix A with the ranked lists and the coverage figures). Two Books, One Investor is history — the cross-analysis of the two prototypes whose defects became segments E to M of Appendix B — and is not needed to build. The prototypes, growize-lead-console.html and growize-investor-portal.html, remain the reference for screens, copy and queues; their data layer is replaced by what is here.

If you are starting the build. §17 phase 0 is the first day’s work, and two things in it have dates that are not yours to move: the Zoho licence lapses on 13 September 2026, and the securities-law opinion (Q20) gates scale. Everything else follows the phases.

Conventions. A fact has exactly one writer; the writer is named on every table in this document. A hint orders somebody’s day and moves no state. A gate is a rung the IR cannot pass until a Finance fact exists. The mirror is Supabase; the book is Zoho. Anything marked open is a decision not yet taken, with the default this document assumes.

The one rule. Zoho is where a fact is written; Supabase is where it is read; the portal writes Zoho as the signed-in person and writes the mirror from Zoho’s answer in the same request; the investor app never touches Zoho; nothing is written in two places by two hands.

Why this shape

Zoho is primary because the founder wants the CRM to be the staff’s working system on honest, per-user seats; that is a legitimate design once each human holds a seat and acts as themselves. Supabase is the read model because the Zoho API is built for integration, not for interactive screens: twenty concurrent calls per org, a credit budget, and hundreds of milliseconds per call. The investor app already lives on Supabase, so its side of the mirror already exists. Two of each because the leads population is ten times the investor population, must stay analysable whole, must never hold identity data, and must keep working when the investor side is down — and the reverse.

The known cost. Zoho holds the ledger behind a licence; the API budget is finite (50,000 credits a day plus 1,000 per seat, 20 concurrent); Zoho’s audit log records writes but not views; and anyone who can write through the portal can also write in Zoho’s own UI. Sections 10, 14 and 15 are the answers to each.

Identity, seats and access

staff are Zoho people; investors are Supabase people

Staff sign in with Zoho

The portals authenticate against Zoho Accounts with OAuth 2.0 authorization code and PKCE, on the India data centre: https://accounts.zoho.in/oauth/v2/auth with access_type=offline so a refresh token is issued on first consent; the authorization code lives two minutes and is used once. The portal backend (an edge function in the matching Supabase project) exchanges the code, fetches the person’s identity (AaaServer.profile.READ) and their CRM profile and role (ZohoCRM.users.READ), stores the refresh token encrypted under a KMS-wrapped key in staff_tokens (service role only), and issues the portal session: an HttpOnly cookie carrying a signed JWT with zoho_user_id, org, profile, role, expiring in eight hours.

Every Zoho call the portal makes on that person’s behalf uses their token — the official Node SDK in multi-user mode with a per-user token store — so Zoho’s audit log names the real person, and Zoho’s profile decides what they may read and write. Removing a person from Zoho ends their portal access at the next refresh. Staff are not Supabase Auth users; their reads of the mirror go through the portal backend, which filters by the Zoho role.

Why not Supabase Auth for staff. Supabase cannot use Zoho as a sign-in provider directly, and a second identity for the same person is a second thing to offboard. One identity per human: staff in Zoho, investors in Supabase.

Investors sign in with Supabase

Unchanged from today: magic link or OTP in the investors project (request-auth-email with its throttle), RLS-scoped reads, requests through the existing edge functions. Investors never hold a Zoho identity and never need Portal-user licences, because they never touch the book.

Profiles and roles inside Zoho

Profiles carry permissions; roles carry data visibility. In the Leads org: profile IR (create/edit own Leads and Touches, no delete, no export, no mass actions), Manager (plus change owner within the team), Admin. Role hierarchy by team so an IR sees only their own records (data sharing set to private; managers see subordinates). Temporary access

A grant is a row — who, what, until when, granted by whom — in staff_grants; the portal shows the extra pages while the row is live and logs every action with the grant id; in Zoho the equivalent is a temporary profile change made by an administrator, logged by Zoho. No shared logins, ever.

The write path

one request, in this order, every time

A staff action

1. UI → portal backend (edge function) with the session cookie and a client-minted event_id
2. backend validates the seat and the input (the friendly version of the rule)
3. backend calls Zoho AS THE USER (SDK multi-user token store) — Zoho applies the blueprint,
   validation rules and field-level security; a refusal comes back as a refusal
4. on success, backend upserts zoho_mirror.<module> from Zoho's response (same request)
5. backend appends events (event_id, actor, payload) and enqueues outbox rows if anything
   else must know (the other project, the app, S3)
6. backend responds; the UI re-reads the mirror and sees its own write

If step 4 fails after step 3 succeeded, the backend enqueues resync(zoho_id) and still responds with success; the mirror heals within a minute and Zoho remains the truth. If step 3 fails, nothing is written anywhere. Retries carry the same event_id, which is a unique field on every custom module, so a retried create cannot double-create.

An edit made inside Zoho’s own UI

A workflow rule on every mirrored module fires a webhook (workflow webhooks do not expire; notification-API channels do) to zoho-webhook, which logs it in webhook_log, dedupes on the idempotency key, and upserts the mirror. The nightly reconcile (Bulk Read of records modified in the last 48 hours, compared with the mirror) catches anything the webhook missed and raises one alert with a list. This path exists because seats make the Zoho UI a door; policy keeps it closed for daily work, the webhook keeps the mirror honest when it is opened.

The read path

fast, scoped, and honest about its age

The console reads the leads mirror through the portal backend, filtered by the Zoho role (own leads, team leads), from the materialised views; a single record can be refreshed from Zoho on demand when the person asks (one API call), never a list.

The portal reads the investors mirror the same way; identity fields are never in the mirror, so a reveal is a live Zoho read as the user (section 14). Queues — waiting on Finance, the KAM’s day, Compliance’s items — are views over the mirror.

The app reads its RLS-scoped views directly, as today. Every money or holding figure carries synced_at; when the newest relevant mirror row is older than app_config.stale_after_minutes the figure is replaced by “being updated”. Because portal writes go through, the only facts that can be stale are edits made in Zoho’s UI — which is why those are discouraged.

Why not read Zoho live. Twenty concurrent calls per org and hundreds of milliseconds each. A list screen for fifteen people would queue behind itself; the mirror answers in milliseconds and keeps answering when Zoho is down.

Documents and signatures

eMudhra signs; Zoho stores; the app fetches on demand

Templates live in emSigner (NDA, supplementary agreement, allocation letter, FEMA declaration, receipts); the template version is stored on the Document row. Sending: the portal’s sign-send function creates the emSigner request (template, recipient email and mobile, signing mode) as the organisation’s API key, and the Document row records who asked, so Zoho attributes it to the person. eMudhra emails and messages the investor directly. Signing modes: Aadhaar eSign (OTP to the Aadhaar-linked mobile; two credits per signing under Zoho Sign’s pricing — confirm eMudhra’s) for residents; Class 3 DSC for NRIs; wet ink only for a power of attorney. Completion: emSigner’s callback (or a poll every ten minutes if the callback is unavailable — confirm in their API) hits sign-webhook; the integration user sets state = signed, uploads the signed PDF and the audit trail as attachments on the Document, stores the SHA-256 of the PDF, and the gate function informs the Leads org.

Verification is Finance looking at the copy and clicking verify — a human check of the right document, not of the signature, which the provider already proved. Storage: the signed copy lives in Zoho as an attachment (and, if you prefer WorkDrive, there); Supabase holds metadata and the hash only. The app shows a document by calling document-fetch, which streams the attachment from Zoho with a one-minute signed link and never keeps a copy at rest. E-stamping precedes signing for instruments that attract duty; the certificate number sits on the Document row and sign-send refuses a template that needs duty without it.

The trade you accepted. Documents only in Zoho means every document view depends on the Zoho licence and API budget. If that ever bites, the fallback is a copy in Supabase Storage behind signed URLs — one flag in document-fetch, no schema change.

The NDA is the one document that lives in the Leads org: system-sent from the console’s “NDA requested” under Finance’s template, verified by eMudhra’s event, attached to the Lead, copied once to the Contact at handover.

Money

receipts, holds, lapses, refunds, payouts

Arrival. With virtual accounts, the bank’s credit webhook creates an unmatched Receipt on the right Contact; without them, Finance Ops records the receipt from the statement, and the row is unmatched until someone else matches it. Matching is the Head of Finance (or the bank feed) setting matched_by and matched_at; the validation rule refuses the same hand; matching is what confirms the money and what opens the gate. The advance opens a Hold (30 days), reserves units on the shelf and opens the app account, tentative. The balance makes the mark permanent (formula) and stamps permanent_at once. Lapse is a Head of Finance or BU Owner action after the deadline: it writes a forfeit Receipt and a refund Receipt (unmatched until the bank debit event), frees the shelf and closes the app account. Refunds match from the bank’s debit event. Payouts to a partner are their share of profit (section 16, G9) and go to the beneficiary registered with the bank; the app shows them from the mirror of a Payouts module or from Zoho Books, whichever the finance team runs — open, default: a Payouts module in the Investor org.

Maker-checker, always. No receipt confirms money by the click that records it. The bank feed counts as the second hand.

Six alerts, to a phone, and no others

  • Outbox stalled — any row with attempts ≥ 3 or older than 15 minutes undelivered.
  • Webhook failures — a dead-lettered inbound event, or a signature that failed to verify.
  • Mirror stale — reconcile found diffs, or a module’s newest synced_at is older than the threshold.

Email, uploads, templates and automation

added 4 September — three asks, each possible because every person now has a Zoho identity

Email sent from the portal, as the person

Zoho CRM’s Send Mail API — POST /crm/v8/{module}/{record_id}/actions/send_mail, scope ZohoCRM.send_mail.all.CREATE — sends from the signed-in user’s configured mailbox (their Zoho Mail address, an alias, or an organisation address) and associates the email with the Lead or Contact by record id, so it appears on the record’s Emails list in Zoho and in the person’s own Sent folder. The console’s “Intro email sent” and the portal’s “supplementary sent” stop being records of something done elsewhere and become the act itself: one click composes from a template, sends as the person, logs the touch with the message id, and the mirror shows it. Replies land on the record through the person’s mailbox association in Zoho.

Limits to design around. The API allows a maximum of 100 emails a day per user; attachments travel as Files-API ids and may not exceed 10 MB in total; the person’s email must be configured in CRM (native with Zoho Mail); recipients must not be bounced or blocked; and the consent field gates the button — no consent, no send. Bulk sends are not this path: a campaign is a Zoho campaign on the marketing-consented segment, never a loop over the Send Mail API.

Templates, chosen at send time

Templates are Zoho CRM email templates per module, with merge fields (${Leads.First Name}, ${Contacts.ARL Code}, ${Contacts.Hold Deadline}), owned and edited in Zoho by an administrator (or by the Head of Finance for Finance’s own), listed in the portal through the settings API, and picked from a list that shows only the templates the person’s role may send. Passing a template id fills subject, content and attachments; anything the person types on top overrides the template for that one message. Every template has an owner, a version, an audience rule and a purpose (service or marketing).

Automation that makes the day shorter, without lying about the count

Zoho workflow rules send template emails on events with no human in the loop: acknowledgments on matched receipts, hold reminders, the welcome words, the FEMA and MCA clocks to the seats that own them. Three rules keep it honest. Automated emails are logged as system touches, never as the IR’s human touches — the count of real conversations is the measure the business trusts, and a robot must not inflate it. Nothing automated carries money or identity — no account numbers, no PAN, no attachments of signed instruments; the agreement goes through eMudhra with its e-stamp, not through an email rule. Every automated message has an owner, a template version and an audience rule, and the investor can withdraw from anything that is not a service notification. Zoho Cadences (sequenced emails and reminders) are available for the IR’s chase; default off — if switched on, email steps run as system touches and calls remain a person’s task on their day.

Why through Zoho and not a separate mailer. The person already has the identity, the mailbox and the record in Zoho; a separate mailer would mean a second sender identity, a second log and a second place for consent. The app’s transactional mail (invites, magic links) stays with the app’s mailer, because the app’s users are not Zoho people.

The repository

added 8 September · one repo, folders not submodules, and the guards that run on every push

Every failure this book fears is one copy of a fact drifting from another copy. That is what §11 is about, what the mapper is about, what the reconcile is about. It would be strange to spend twenty-five sections on that and then split the code into four repositories, which is the same problem again wearing git's clothes.

One repository. Folders, not submodules. A change to contracts/ that touches the console, the portal and the app is one commit — in or out, never half-landed. Split into four repositories it is four pull requests that can merge in any order, and the window between the first and the last is exactly the window in which the seam is broken and nobody knows. A submodule does not fix this; a submodule is a second repository wearing a folder's clothes, with its own pointer to keep in sync — the same drift, now hidden behind a commit hash nobody reads.

The trade, stated. One repository means one CI run for a change that touches one folder, and a clone that carries the console's history even if you only work on the app. At this size — three applications, two people — that costs seconds. It buys atomicity, which cannot be bought later.

The daily diagnostic

added 8 September · it explains, and never fixes

§22 counts fifty-three failure modes that are silent — the system keeps serving pages while a number is quietly wrong. The eleven canaries page you when a specific thing goes bad. This is the other half: at 07:00 Asia/Kolkata every day, something walks the whole system, writes down what it found, and explains it. It does not touch anything.

The one promise. The diagnostic never applies a fix. It is read-only by construction — every client it builds comes from a read-only credential, and CI fails the build if a --dry-run leaves the working tree different from how it found it. A finding is an issue on GitHub, never a commit to the system it is describing.

Why so strict. A job that both detects and repairs will one day repair the wrong thing at three in the morning, and the record of what the system actually looked like before it "helped" will be gone. Worse: it removes the human from exactly the decisions this book spent twenty-five sections putting a named human in front of. Recording money is always allowed and matching is gated (D21); reversals need two hands (D22). A robot that quietly matched a receipt because it looked obvious would break both, and it would break them invisibly.

G · The law

9 items

Added 4 September. The software design is lawful; the biggest legal questions sit in the instrument itself and in filings no software can make for you. Not legal advice — each line names the statute so counsel can confirm it.

G1
Get a written opinion on what a “unit” is
Section 11AA(2) of the SEBI Act makes a scheme a collective investment scheme when contributions are pooled and used for the scheme, made to receive profits, income, produce or property, managed on behalf of investors, and the investors have no day-to-day control — the four are all visible on the face of a farm-unit programme, and the regime was born from 1990s agro and plantation schemes. Partner capital in an LLP is outside the BUDS Act’s “deposit” (s. 2(4)(e)); whether the LLP structure also takes the units outside 11AA is a securities-law opinion, not a software setting.
